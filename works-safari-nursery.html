Safari Nursery

Driving retention through emotional engagement: +3.5pp Day 1 Retention · 65–70% Sustained Engagement.

The Challenge

A leading EdTech platform with millions of users faced a familiar growth problem: high early engagement, but low retention. While "Safari Workshop" was a top destination for new users on Day 0, the drop-off after their first session was significant.

The opportunity was clear: turn a popular feature into a habit-forming experience that drives repeat visits and long-term loyalty.

My Role & Approach

As Product Design Lead, I led a cross-functional team through a 4-sprint cycle to design, build, and validate a new feature — the "Nursery" — within Safari Workshop.

My focus was not just on creating a fun experience, but on addressing a business challenge: how might we reduce the drop-off after Day 0 by transforming a popular destination into a retention driver?

Discovery & Hypothesis

Data signals:

  • Safari Workshop had high Day 0 traffic, but users weren't returning.
  • The existing experience lacked emotional hooks or progression systems.

Hypothesis:

"If we introduce a caretaker-driven experience with emotional connection and progressive discovery, we will increase Day 1 retention by creating a reason for users to return."

Design Strategy

Safari Nursery was built around a core loop anchored in three behavioral drivers:

  1. Emotional investment — Players adopt and care for a baby animal, creating ownership and personal connection.
  2. Progressive discovery — Each visit reveals new information and deepens the bond.
  3. Intrinsic motivation — The experience is playful and rewarding, not transactional.

Why this was a growth play: The design wasn't just about "fun" — it was about habit formation. By giving users a reason to return (checking on their cub, discovering new favorites), we increased the likelihood of Day 1 and Day 7 retention.

Validation & Iteration

We rapidly prototyped the experience using ChatGPT to accelerate the design-to-prototype cycle — generating multiple variations of interface layouts, interaction patterns, and component configurations in days rather than weeks. This allowed us to validate navigation and emotional engagement early, with enough time to iterate before launch.

We then conducted playtests with 20+ target users (children aged 4-7). The sessions validated our navigation approach and revealed opportunities to deepen emotional connection through visual distinctiveness (collars, fur colors) and exaggerated character reactions.

Results

Engagement:

  • 33% of Safari visitors engaged with the Nursery at launch.
  • Within a week, that grew to 68%, and stabilized at 65–70% over 4 months.

Retention (the numbers that mattered):

"Users who engaged with the Nursery were +3.5 percentage points more likely to return on Day 1 compared to those who only visited Safari (controlling for Day 0 activity)."

Business implication: Retention is a critical metric for subscription-based EdTech products. A +3.5pp lift in D1 retention represents a meaningful increase in user lifetime value — and a clear signal that the design intervention had impact.

Key Learnings

  1. Emotional hooks drive retention. Users don't just return for content — they return for connection.
  2. Small interventions can have outsized impact. A 3.5pp lift came from a focused, low-cost design change.
  3. Data-informed design is not just about dashboards. It's about forming clear hypotheses and validating them through both quantitative and qualitative feedback.

Next Steps

  • Integrate AI-driven personalization — recommending activities and content based on each user's interaction history and preferences.
  • Extend the Nursery model to other animal species within the Safari feature, creating a cohesive ecosystem of caretaking experiences.
  • Introduce growth mechanics — allowing baby animals to evolve over time based on the user's engagement and care efforts, deepening emotional investment and long-term retention.
