UX Intervention for a Legal Provider Web App

Rescuing a stalled legal tech platform by restructuring its information architecture and conducting usability testing to successfully unblock project delivery.

My Role

Product Designer

Timeline

Sep – Oct 2020

Services

UX Intervention · Information Architecture · Usability Testing

Key Outcome

Unblocked Project Delivery

Discuss a project

The Challenge

RayNa Corp optimizes complex administrative workflows for modern law firms. While a third-party development partner spent a year architecting their core legal Web App, critical friction issues arose prior to launch. The system wasn't bottlenecked by code defects, but by major user experience gaps that severely threatened client satisfaction.

With development stalled and executive stakeholders dissatisfied, the objective required a strategic UX intervention: diagnostic friction points needed identification, and an intuitive interface solution had to be deployed rapidly within the existing development framework to secure client alignment.

My Role & Approach

Stepping into an in-progress cycle as the Product Designer, I prioritized neutralizing stakeholder friction. My methodology centered on executing a lightweight UI audit, bridging complex engineering environment limitations with user expectations, and formulating a validated dashboard blueprint without forcing an expensive backend overhaul.

Heuristic Discovery & User Flow Audit

I executed a preliminary heuristic audit across the transactional layout to map structural issues slowing down internal legal operations. The diagnostic phase isolated clear UX gaps requiring immediate structural changes:

  • Transforming text-entry fields into standardized geographical dropdown protocols to eliminate data fragmentation.
  • Accelerating internal legal matching flows through global search algorithms, advanced filtering, and list sorting.
  • Deploying explicit status tracking mechanisms and visual feedback systems to ensure workflow alignment.

User Research & Workspace Empathy

To validate these early insights, we conducted workspace interviews via Zoom and analyzed user behavioral routines recorded on Loom. Gathering direct feedback from diverse administrative legal professionals surfaced the core requirements needed to transform fragmented client legacy tools into high-adoption dashboards:

Core Ecosystem Requirements Matrix:

  • Law Firms & Admins: High-efficiency portals to request updates, manage patient folders, and track transaction history logs.
  • Providers: Integrated search frameworks, multi-column sorting (State + Provider Type), and instant database import mechanics.
  • Visual Indicators: Low-friction, color-coded verification statuses to manage multi-provider legal claims.

Architecture Restructuring & Strategy

We restructured the product's information architecture to seamlessly align large volumes of administrative data with user mental models. Benchmarking data-heavy enterprise platforms led to the implementation of optimized data tables featuring explicit micro-copy instructions, alert dialogs to mitigate data loss, and color-coded status timelines (traffic light hierarchy) that dramatically lowered user hesitation.

Low-Fidelity Prototyping & Iteration

Operating under tight engineering deadlines, I built comprehensive Low-Fidelity prototypes in Adobe XD to isolate interaction mechanics before investing in high-fidelity visual styling. Running internal validation cycles with the Project Manager and Lead Developer ensured that every design layout remained 100% viable within the active codebase constraints.

Subsequent usability tests with real RayNa Corp users unlocked invaluable iteration vectors. We simplified verification parameters into 3 concise states (Verified within the year, Verified over a year ago, Never verified) and transformed multi-name fields into dynamic UI tags, ensuring optimal usability before moving to final handoff.

Validated Outcomes & Handoff

The Impact: After months of being completely stalled, the redesigned framework unblocked the entire development velocity loop. The client enthusiastically approved the structured LoFi user experience blueprints with zero high-fidelity visual overhead required—saving valuable product delivery cycles.

I executed a seamless technical handoff by supplying the development team with two finalized interactive prototypes (Law Firm Portal & Provider Workspace), fully compliant with US accessibility standards (WCAG). This strategic alignment successfully turned a high-friction crisis into a highly valued product delivery.
